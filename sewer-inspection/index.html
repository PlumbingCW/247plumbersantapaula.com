Sewer Inspection in Santa Paula, CA

See inside the pipe before you pay for a repair. Sewer camera inspection in Santa Paula, CA, with a clear price before work starts.

Call (805) 249-7135

A sewer inspection sends a waterproof camera through your drain or sewer line and records what is inside. In Santa Paula, CA we use it for recurring clogs, pre-purchase checks, after a root cleaning and before a repair or lining job.

The picture answers questions that guesses cannot. Is the pipe cracked or just dirty? Are the roots at one joint or along the line? Is a low spot holding water? Is it clay, cast iron or plastic?

Because the pipe is buried, a camera is the only practical way to know. It also gives you a recording to compare later.

Before buying a home

A home inspection usually does not include the sewer line. A camera pass before closing can reveal roots, breaks or old Orangeburg pipe that is expensive to replace. It is useful in older neighborhoods around downtown Main Street where lines are older.

Ask the seller whether the line has been cleaned or repaired, and share the recording with your agent.

After repeat clogs

If a drain keeps clogging after cleaning, a camera finds the reason. See our drain cleaning page for the first steps, and sewer line repair for what we do when the pipe is damaged.

How the inspection works

  1. Access

    We find a cleanout, vent or fixture that opens the line.

  2. Run the camera

    A flexible rod pushes the camera and shows distance.

  3. Record

    We mark the locations of problems and save the video.

  4. Explain

    We walk through the footage and what it means.

Locating the pipe

Cameras with a locator transmitter let us find the pipe and mark its depth at the surface. That helps plan a repair that avoids driveways and landscaping where possible.

What an inspection cannot do

A camera shows the inside of the pipe. It cannot always tell if the pipe will fail next month. Where the footage is unclear, we say so instead of guessing.

Reading the camera findings

The video shows the inside of the pipe in real time. We point out the pipe material, the joints, offsets, root intrusion, cracks, sags where water pools and any buildup. A sag, often called a belly, collects waste and clogs again and again, which is why a line that has been cleared three times may need more than another cleaning.

We note the distance to each finding from the cleanout, so a repair, if needed, starts at the right spot instead of guessing along the line. Ask for a copy of the recording and keep it with your property records.

Cleaning and inspection often go together. If the camera finds grease or roots, see sewer line cleaning. If it finds a break, see sewer line repair.
