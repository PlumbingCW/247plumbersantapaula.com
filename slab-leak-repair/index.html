Emergency Slab Leak Repair in Santa Paula, CA

Hot spot on the floor, running meter or the sound of water under the house? Emergency slab leak repair in Santa Paula, CA, 24/7.

Call (805) 249-7135

A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs under or inside a concrete foundation. In Santa Paula, CA, homes built on slabs can develop slab leaks as old copper corrodes or as lines rub against rock and concrete.

You can usually tell something is wrong before you see water. The water bill climbs, the meter spins with everything off, a spot on the floor feels warm, or you hear running water in a quiet house.

Because the pipe is under the floor, the first job is to confirm the leak and find it accurately. Guessing means cutting concrete in the wrong place.

Slab leak signs that need a call now

A slab leak will not stop on its own, and water under a foundation can do damage you cannot see. We answer 24/7, including nights and weekends, and offer same-day appointments when available.

If you hear water running and every fixture is off, shut the main valve and call. A warm floor spot suggests a hot water line is the culprit.

While you wait

  • Turn off the main water valve to stop the leak.
  • Turn off the water heater if the hot line is leaking, so it does not run dry.
  • Check the meter again after thirty minutes to see if it has stopped.
  • Note which rooms have warm spots, damp flooring or musty smells.

How we confirm a slab leak

We start with a pressure test and a meter check to confirm that water is leaking. Then we use electronic listening equipment to narrow down the location. The goal is to mark one small spot before any floor is opened.

Spot repair or reroute

Cutting a small hole in the slab and fixing the pipe is the fastest way to stop a single leak. If the line is old and likely to leak again, rerouting avoids the slab altogether. We describe both options and the tradeoffs before you decide.

What causes slab leaks

  • Corrosion of copper pipe over many years.
  • Abrasion where pipe moves against concrete or rock.
  • High water pressure that stresses weak points.
  • Soil movement that shifts the slab and pipe.

Ask your water utility what pressure to expect at your meter, and we can check it at the house with a gauge.

After the repair

Flooring over the repair, such as tile, hardwood or carpet, is handled by a flooring contractor. We patch the concrete. If water has been under the house for a while, mention it to your insurance carrier and ask about moisture testing.

Opening a slab and replacing or rerouting pipe falls under the California Plumbing Code. Ask the City of Santa Paula building department about permits before a reroute starts.
