Kitchen Sink Plumbing With a Garbage Disposal in Santa Paula

Under a double sink with a disposal, a few pipes do a lot of work. Here is how they fit together and where DIY installs go wrong.

Call (805) 249-7135

By the Santa Paula 24/7 Plumbing team · Guide for Santa Paula homeowners · 2026.

The space under a kitchen sink looks like a tangle, but it follows a simple order. Water goes down the drain, through the disposal if you have one, out an outlet pipe, through a trap and into the wall. The dishwasher joins that path partway down.

Understanding the order makes it easier to install a disposal, replace one or fix a leak. It also helps you decide whether a job is a Saturday project or one for a plumber.

If the sink is leaking now, put a bowl under it, close the shutoff valves and read the section on leaks.

The path water takes

  1. Sink basket and flange

    The basket strainer or the disposal flange seals the drain opening.

  2. Disposal

    The unit hangs under the flange and grinds food waste.

  3. Outlet and drain arm

    A short pipe leaves the disposal and meets the second basin.

  4. Trap

    A curved pipe holds water to block sewer gas.

  5. Wall drain

    The trap connects to the line in the wall.

Double sink with a disposal

In a two basin sink, one basin has the disposal and the other has a basket strainer. A tailpiece from the second basin joins the disposal's outlet pipe with a continuous waste tee, and the combined line runs to a single trap. The lower the pieces sit, the harder it is to keep a slope toward the wall.

A common DIY mistake is stacking two traps or leaving long flat runs where water pools. Either one makes the sink drain slowly and smell.

The dishwasher connection

A dishwasher drain hose joins the disposal at a small inlet, or the sink drain above the trap if there is no disposal. The inlet has a plug that must be knocked out when a disposal is first connected, which is easy to forget. A forgotten plug makes the dishwasher drain slowly or not at all.

The hose is usually looped high under the counter or fitted with an air gap to keep dirty water from running back. Check your dishwasher instructions for the arrangement it needs.

Electrical and mounting

A disposal needs power, usually from an outlet under the sink controlled by a switch. If your sink has no switch or outlet, someone has to add them. The mounting ring locks the unit to the flange with a twist, and it must be seated fully so it does not drip at the top.

See garbage disposal repair for jams and leaks, and faucet repair if the faucet and supply lines are also old.

When to call a plumber

Call if you are adding a disposal where none exists, if the drain is slow after installation, if the slope is wrong or if a leak is behind the wall. For a clog beyond the trap, see drain cleaning. We offer same-day appointments when available.

Choosing a disposal

Disposals are rated by horsepower. A smaller unit handles light use and a larger one handles heavier use with less jamming and noise. Continuous feed units run while you add scraps. Batch feed units run only when a stopper is in place. Pick based on your household and how much noise you will accept.

Check the size of the sink opening, the clearance in the cabinet and whether the unit fits your existing trap arrangement.

Air gap or high loop

Dishwasher drains must be arranged so dirty sink water cannot flow back into the dishwasher. Some areas use an air gap fitting at the sink and some accept a high loop under the counter. Follow your dishwasher manual and ask the City of Santa Paula building department what applies.

Keeping the disposal healthy

  • Run cold water before, during and after use.
  • Avoid grease, large bones, fibrous scraps and pits.
  • Grind small amounts at a time.
  • Do not pour chemical cleaners into it.
  • Clean it with ice and citrus peels or a safe cleaner now and then.

Parts and tools checklist

  • The disposal, mounting kit and power cord or a hardwire kit.
  • Plumber's putty or the gasket supplied with the unit.
  • A new trap, tailpiece and slip nut washers.
  • A dishwasher drain hose and clamp.
  • Basic tools, a bucket and a flashlight.

Buy a few extra washers and a slip joint extension. Nothing slows a job like a missing five dollar part, and a trip to the store with the sink disconnected is no fun.

Kitchen sinks in Santa Paula homes

Older kitchens in the homes around the historic downtown often have older drain lines in the wall, and cast iron or steel drain stubs can corrode where they meet new plastic pipe. When a sink is replaced, ask the plumber to check the wall stub.

Ask the City of Santa Paula building department whether a permit applies to a kitchen remodel. We also serve kitchens in Ojai and Ventura.
