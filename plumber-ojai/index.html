Ojai sits at about 745 feet in the Ojai Valley, with Los Padres National Forest to the north. The city incorporated in 1921 and had 7,637 residents in 2020. Most of its homes were built between 1940 and 1980, which tells us a lot before we ever open a wall.

Water in the Ojai Valley

Casitas Municipal Water District serves an area that includes the City of Ojai, and its water comes from Lake Casitas. Ask your own provider who bills you, since retail service differs by address.

Hardness and pressure vary, so test your tap and ask your water provider for its quality report. Scale shortens the life of tank water heaters, so see water heater repair and water softener and filtration.

Emergency plumber in Ojai: first steps

  • Close the main water valve, then open the lowest faucet.
  • Turn off the water heater if a hot line is leaking.
  • Switch off power to any outlets near standing water if you can reach the panel safely.
  • Take photos for your insurance carrier and call us.

Our line is open 24/7, so a night or weekend leak gets the same answer as a weekday one. We offer same-day appointments when available.

Downtown Ojai and older commercial buildings

Downtown Ojai has a Spanish-style arcade and the Libbey Park area, with older commercial buildings and small restaurants. Older building drains clog with grease and scale, and restrooms and kitchens need quick help when they fail. See hydro jetting and commercial plumbing.

Returning home after an evacuation

Los Padres National Forest borders Ojai to the north, so wildfire evacuations are a real part of life in the valley. Homes can sit empty for days. When you return, open faucets slowly, check under sinks and around the water heater, and look at the meter with everything off.

A meter that still moves means a leak somewhere. See leak detection and call us if you need help finding it.
