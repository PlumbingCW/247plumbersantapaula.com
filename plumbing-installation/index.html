Plumbing installation covers new fixtures, relocated lines and appliance hookups in homes and small businesses. In Santa Paula, CA, the work ranges from swapping a toilet to running new supply and drain lines for a bathroom remodel or accessory dwelling.

Installation goes best when plumbing is planned before the walls are closed. Where the toilet drain goes, how far a sink is from the vent and where the water heater sits are all decisions that are hard to change later.

Bathroom and kitchen remodels

In a remodel, plumbing happens twice. First comes rough-in, where supply, drain and vent lines are placed before walls close. Then comes trim-out, where fixtures are set and tested. Rough-in decisions are the ones to settle early.

If you are moving a sink or a toilet, we check the drain slope and the vent path, because those set how far fixtures can move. A short measuring visit before you order fixtures saves rework.

Accessory dwellings and additions

Adding a unit or a room means new fixtures and a tie-in to existing lines. Permits and inspections are involved. Ask the City of Santa Paula building department which approvals apply to your plan, and we work with the inspection schedule.

Appliance hookups

Dishwashers, ice makers, washing machines and gas appliances each have their own connection needs. We install shutoff valves and connectors that are meant for the appliance. Gas work follows its own rules, so confirm scope when you call.

Before you buy fixtures

  • Measure the rough-in distance for toilets, usually from the wall to the drain center.
  • Check that the faucet matches the number of sink holes.
  • Confirm the water heater fits the closet and the vent.
  • Keep the model numbers and manuals for the installer.

If an installation reveals old pipe in poor condition, we say so before we connect new fixtures to it. See repiping if the lines behind the wall are worn.

How an installation visit goes

  1. Review the plan

    We look at the space, the fixture specs and where the supply and drain connections are.

  2. Shut off and protect

    Water is shut off and the work area is covered.

  3. Set and connect

    Fixtures are set, connected to shutoffs and drains and checked for leaks.

  4. Test and walk through

    We run every fixture, check pressure and show you where the shutoffs are.

Gas and electrical limits

Some installations touch gas or electrical work, such as a gas water heater or a disposal that needs a new outlet. Say so when you call, and we tell you which parts we handle and which need another licensed trade. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Santa Paula building department what your project needs.

A clean installation also leaves the next repair easier. We label shutoffs, leave access panels where we can and give you the model and part numbers for your records.

California adopts its plumbing code as part of Title 24, and it sets minimum rules for fixtures, traps and venting. Your local building department decides how it applies to your remodel.
