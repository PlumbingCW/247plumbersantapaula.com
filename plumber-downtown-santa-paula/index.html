Downtown Santa Paula is the oldest part of the city. It is centered on Main Street, and it holds the city's oldest homes. Santa Paula was laid out in the 1870s, incorporated in 1902, and the railroad arrived in 1887. Houses, small apartment buildings and shops here share the same few blocks.

If you own a home or a storefront downtown, call (805) 249-7135 and tell us the street and roughly how old the building is. That one detail helps us plan the visit. This page explains what we know about the area and how we approach plumbing in buildings of different ages.

What the area looks like

A Ventura County cultural heritage survey of Santa Paula looked at the blocks around Santa Paula Street, Pleasant Street, Mill Street and Seventh through Tenth Streets. It recorded Victorian cottages, Italianate and Queen Anne houses from the 1890s, Colonial Revival homes and a large number of California bungalows built from about 1908 to 1920. Most of the homes in the surveyed area dated from the 1890s through the 1940s.

Along Virginia Terrace and Glen Way the survey noted Spanish Colonial Revival and English Tudor houses from the 1920s and 1930s. The survey is not recent, so it describes the housing as it was when it was written. Many homes have been remodeled since, and some blocks have changed. It tells you the era of the buildings, not what is inside the walls today.

What building age does and does not tell you

A house built a century ago may still have some of its original pipes, or it may have been repiped in part. The age of the building cannot tell you which. Past owners may have replaced a kitchen line, added a bathroom or patched a drain, and those changes are rarely written down.

That is why we look before we recommend. A sewer camera inspection shows the real condition of a drain line on video. A leak detection visit finds a hidden leak without cutting into plaster that is hard to match. If the supply lines turn out to be worn, we explain the options, including repiping, and you decide.

Main Street shops and restaurants

The blocks around Main Street also hold small businesses. Restroom leaks, a backed-up floor drain or a slow kitchen line can stop a shop from opening for the day. We handle these through commercial plumbing. Tell us the type of business and what the drain or fixture is doing when you call.

Before you plan a renovation

Renovating a house in an older part of town raises questions about permits and any review that applies. Plumbing rules and permit requirements change, and we do not guess at them. Ask the City of Santa Paula building department what applies to your address before work begins. We are happy to describe the plumbing side of the job so you can bring clear questions.

Get ready for the plumber downtown

A few minutes of preparation makes the visit smoother.

Find the main shutoff

Locate the valve before you need it. In older buildings it can be inside a cabinet or behind an access panel.

Note any past work

Mention remodels, new bathrooms or earlier repairs, even if you are not sure of the dates.

Clear the access

Move items away from sinks, the water heater and any cleanout.

Describe the symptom

Write down what you saw or heard and when it began.
