How to Do Plumbing for a Shower: What Santa Paula Homes Need

A shower looks simple from the outside. Behind the tile are five parts that must line up before the walls are closed.

Call (805) 249-7135

By the Santa Paula 24/7 Plumbing team · Guide for Santa Paula homeowners · 2026.

A shower has a few moving parts and a lot of things that must not move after the tile is on. The valve, the supply pipes, the drain, the vent and the waterproof layer all need to be right, because fixing any of them later means taking the shower apart.

This guide explains the layout for a typical remodel. It helps you ask the right questions before you hire a plumber or a tile setter. It does not replace plans or a permit, and rules vary, so ask the City of Santa Paula building department what applies to your project.

If you are only replacing a fixture, a call with the model number is often enough.

The valve and where it goes

The valve sits in the wall, usually between knee and shoulder height so you can reach it without stepping into the spray. It is set to the finished wall depth, which depends on the tile you choose. If the depth is wrong, the trim will not fit, so decide on tile and trim before the valve is set.

Install an access panel or leave the valve reachable from the back. A valve that has to be replaced through tile is costly. See faucet and valve repair for what a cartridge failure looks like.

Drain, trap and vent

A shower drain needs a trap to block sewer gas, and it needs a vent so the water drains smoothly. The drain slopes toward the main line, and the shower floor slopes toward the drain. If the drain cannot be reached without cutting a floor joist or a slab, the layout may need to change.

Slab homes and homes over a crawlspace are different. A slab means cutting concrete. A crawlspace means working from below. Tell us which you have. See plumbing installation.

Waterproofing

Tile and grout are not waterproof. Water gets through them and must be stopped behind. A shower pan liner, a membrane or a pre-formed pan sits under the tile and slopes to the drain. A flood test, where the pan is filled and left to sit, shows if it holds.

Ask your tile setter and plumber who coordinates the pan and the drain, since a poorly sealed drain collar is a common leak.

Water pressure and flow

A multi-head shower uses more water. Check that your supply pipes can deliver it. Older homes with galvanized pipe may not, see repiping. A water heater that is too small runs out quickly with a large shower.

Questions to ask before you start

  • Is the shower on a slab, a crawlspace or an upper floor?
  • Where can the drain and vent connect?
  • What waterproofing method will be used and who tests it?
  • Does the project need a permit or inspection?

Choosing a valve type

A pressure balancing valve keeps temperature steady when someone flushes a toilet or runs a tap elsewhere. A thermostatic valve lets you set a precise temperature and often controls several outlets. Thermostatic valves cost more and need more room in the wall.

Pick the valve before the tile arrives. The trim kit, the plaster guard and the rough depth all depend on it, and the tile setter needs the final dimensions.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Setting the valve before the tile thickness is known.
  • Forgetting an access panel to the valve.
  • Burying a slip fitting inside a closed wall.
  • Skipping the flood test on the shower pan.
  • Choosing fixtures that need more flow than the supply provides.

Who does what, and in what order

The usual order is demolition, rough plumbing, inspection if required, backer board and waterproofing, flood test, tile, then trim. The plumber returns after tile to install the trim. Agree on this sequence in writing, so nobody closes a wall before the plumbing is tested.

Comfort and accessibility choices

Decide early on a handheld shower, a second head or a bench. A bench or grab bar needs solid blocking inside the wall, which must be added before the wall closes. A handheld needs its own outlet and a wall hook at a useful height.

Walk through the shower layout with a tape measure and a chair. Elbows, hip height and reach tell you more than a drawing about where the valve and the controls should go.

Shower remodels in Santa Paula

Many Santa Paula houses near the historic downtown were built long ago and have been remodeled in pieces. Opening a bathroom wall often exposes old galvanized supply pipe or older drain lines that are worth replacing at the same time.

Ask the City of Santa Paula building department what permits apply to a bathroom remodel, and ask your water utility about pressure. We also work on remodels in Fillmore and Ojai.
