Emergency Water Heater Repair in Santa Paula, CA

No hot water, a pilot that will not stay lit, or water around the tank? Emergency water heater repair in Santa Paula, CA, 24/7.

Water heater repair means diagnosing why a tank or tankless unit stopped heating, leaks, or makes noise, and fixing the part at fault when repair makes sense. Santa Paula, CA households call us most often for no hot water, a pilot light that goes out, and a puddle at the base of the tank.

The cause of a cold shower depends on the fuel. A gas unit usually fails at the thermocouple, the gas control valve or the burner. An electric unit usually fails at a heating element or thermostat. A heat pump or hybrid unit adds a compressor and filter that need their own checks.

Most water heaters in the older homes around downtown are in a garage, closet or utility room. Many Santa Paula garages are also where the washer and the car live, so a slow leak can run unnoticed under stored boxes for weeks.

Leaking water heater: what to do first

A tank that is dripping from a fitting is a repair. A tank that is leaking from the body is finished, and the damage is the flooded floor and drywall, not the heater. We answer 24/7, including nights and weekends, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

Tell the dispatcher whether the water is coming from the top fittings, the relief valve pipe or the bottom of the tank. That tells us whether we are bringing parts or a replacement.

Before the plumber arrives

  • Turn the water heater's cold water supply valve clockwise to stop refilling.
  • Set a gas unit to pilot or off and switch an electric unit off at the breaker.
  • Place a bucket under a slow drip and move stored items off the floor.
  • If you smell gas, leave the home and call 911 or your gas utility from outside before you call us.

Pilot light and thermocouple problems

A gas water heater that lights and then goes out after you release the knob almost always has a worn thermocouple. It is a small, inexpensive part and a short repair on a tank that is otherwise healthy. If the pilot will not stay lit even with a good thermocouple, the gas control valve is the next suspect.

Never relight a pilot in a closed space where you smell gas. Open the garage door, leave, and let the gas utility check it first.

Electric elements, thermostats and breakers

Electric tanks have an upper and a lower heating element. If the upper one fails you get no hot water. If the lower one fails you get a short supply that runs out fast. Both are bolted in, test with a meter, and replace in one visit when the tank is sound.

Hybrid and heat pump water heaters

Heat pump units pull heat from the surrounding air, so they need clearance, a clean filter and a condensate drain that is not blocked. Error codes on the display usually name the failing sensor or fan. We read the code, check airflow and the drain, and replace the part when it is a repair.

Repair or replace?

A tank leaking from its body cannot be repaired. A tank with a failed thermocouple, element or thermostat often can. If the unit is old, repeated repairs add up, and our water heater replacement page explains how we decide. A planned swap is on the installation page, and tankless owners should see tankless water heater service.

Seismic bracing

Southern California homeowners are used to thinking about earthquakes, and a tall tank water heater is one of the heavier things in the house that can tip. Ask the City of Santa Paula building department what bracing applies to your unit, and have the straps checked whenever the tank is serviced.

What drives water heater repair cost

The price is stated before we start. These factors decide it:

A thermocouple, a thermostat, an element and a gas control valve are priced very differently.

Parts for older gas and electric tanks vary in availability, and heat pump units use sensors and fans that cost more.

If the tank body is corroded, repairing a fitting does not make financial sense and we say so.

A tank in an attic or tight closet takes longer than one in an open garage.

Venting, a relief valve line or an expansion tank may need work if the old setup is not safe to leave.
